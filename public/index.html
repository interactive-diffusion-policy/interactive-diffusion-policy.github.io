Abstract

Manipulators sharing a workspace with humans must avoid approaching obstacles while completing their tasks. Such avoidance requires obstacle detection, but camera-based detection can be hindered by occlusion. Avoidance can also move the robot outside the demonstration distribution, making task resumption difficult for a learned policy. These challenges call for a method that integrates reactive avoidance with recovery from out-of-distribution states. In this paper, we propose Interactive Diffusion Policy (IDP), which connects task execution, reactive avoidance, and recovery. Diffusion Policy generates a reference target, and a lower level RMPflow controller integrates target tracking and obstacle avoidance using distance information from proximity sensors. Robot mounted capacitive proximity sensors provide local distance information to compensate for camera observation limitations. Sensor experiments confirmed that the proximity sensors supported avoidance even for occluded and transparent objects that a depth camera failed to detect. To resume the task after avoidance, recovery data are collected by human guidance from off-path states caused by actual RMPflow avoidance. These data are used to train the dynamics model of the Latent Policy Barrier (LPB), which guides task resumption after avoidance. Manipulator experiments under various obstacle conditions showed that the robot resumed the task after avoidance and achieved a task success rate of 91.3%. These results show that IDP integrates collision avoidance and task resumption, enabling task execution that considers both safety and task capability in human shared workspaces.
